Featured Publication

Peer-reviewed research on how urban growth is reshaping the wetlands that protect Colombo from flooding.

Urban Ecosystems | Springer | 2025

Land Use and Land Cover Transformation in Urban Wetlands in Colombo District, Sri Lanka

Ranasinghe, R.R. (first author and corresponding author), Wickramasinghe, D., Maltby, E. & Witharana, A. Urban Ecosystems 28, 242 (2025)

  • Analyzed land use and land cover change across three urban wetlands, Thalangama Environmental Protection Area, Bellanwila-Attidiya Wetland and Boralesgamuwa Lake, over two decades (2001–2021).
  • Classified multi-temporal Landsat imagery in ArcGIS and Google Earth Pro to detect spatial and temporal change, validated with field data.
  • Found a consistent pattern of declining vegetation and paddy fields alongside expanding built-up and open areas, with open water at Boralesgamuwa Lake also expanding through hydrological modifications.
  • Showed how protection status, land-use history and infrastructure projects push each wetland along a different transformation path, and recommended stricter land-use regulation, buffer zone protection and community-based conservation.

Conference Abstract | 2022

Land Use Transformation and Ecosystem Services: A Case Study from Thalangama Environmental Protected Area, Sri Lanka

Ranasinghe (first author), Wickramasinghe, Maltby, Withana & Dharmarathne | Postgraduate Institute of Science Research Congress, Sri Lanka, Oct 28-29, 2022, Abstract No. 214

Tracked two decades of land use change (2000-2020) in and around the Thalangama wetland using remote sensing and GIS, and surveyed 50 local residents and 6 national experts on ecosystem services, analyzed with Principal Component Analysis in SPSS. Paddy fields shrank 3.95% and settlements grew 3.72% inside the wetland, while vegetation in the surrounding areas fell 8.14%.

Science Article | Institute of Biology, Sri Lanka (IOBSL) Research Stories

A Study Reports the First-Ever Harmful Algae Species Found in the Indian Ocean around the Southern Coast of Sri Lanka

Story by Rasadari Ranasinghe, Associate Member, Institute of Biology Sri Lanka | Role: Science Writer & Research Communicator

A science communication article translating a 2021 study (Dissanayake, Wickramasinghe & Manage) on harmful diatoms and dinoflagellates along Sri Lanka's southern coast into an accessible research story. Sampling at five sites (Ambalangoda, Hikkaduwa, Dodanduwa, Mirissa and Tangalle) identified 27 diatom and 10 dinoflagellate species, 11 of them potentially harmful, with the highest concentration at Mirissa. The article explains how the harmful species connect to turbidity and phosphorus from human activity, and why long-term coastal water-quality monitoring matters.
